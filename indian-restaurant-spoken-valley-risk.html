Relevant Platform and Restaurant Marketing Policies

Google Business Profile and Maps Accuracy

Local listings should represent the real business rather than an SEO version of the business. The restaurant name should remain Top of India unless a different name is genuinely used by the business in the real world. Search phrases such as “Indian restaurant Spokane Valley” belong in relevant content and descriptions where appropriate; they should not be artificially appended to the business name merely to influence rankings.

Address, phone number, website, operating hours, restaurant category, service options, photographs, and menu information should also remain accurate. A customer using a map listing may make an immediate decision based on whether the restaurant appears open, where it is located, or how to contact it. Incorrect information therefore creates both customer-service and marketing risk.

Review Practices

Customer reviews should reflect genuine customer experiences. Restaurant operators may encourage authentic feedback, but the process should not depend on whether a customer is expected to leave a positive review. Purchased reviews, fabricated reviews, employee-created customer reviews, or systems designed to solicit public feedback only from satisfied customers create trust and platform risk.

Review responses should also remain professional. A response is public brand communication, not a private argument. Staff should avoid disclosing personal customer information, escalating emotional disputes, or making claims that cannot be verified.

Advertising and Promotional Claims

Restaurant advertising should accurately represent current offers. Prices, discounts, limited-time promotions, menu availability, ordering options, dining options, and similar claims should be checked before publication.

Quality language should also be used carefully. Descriptive statements can help customers understand the restaurant, but rankings, awards, or “best” claims should not be invented or presented as independent recognition without evidence.

Menu Representation

Online menu content should help customers understand what they can reasonably expect. Names, descriptions, pricing, availability, ingredients, and dietary designations should be reviewed when they are published or changed.

A menu is particularly sensitive because customers may make purchasing or dietary decisions from it. Marketers should not add dietary guarantees, ingredient claims, or availability statements simply because they improve search relevance.

Food Photography

Images used as representations of Top of India should fairly represent the restaurant. Stock photography can create problems when visitors reasonably interpret it as a photograph of a dish actually prepared by the restaurant. Real food, interior, exterior, and experience photography generally creates clearer entity signals and reduces the risk of customer expectations being shaped by unrelated imagery.

Risk Categories Associated With Misuse

Local Search Risk

Local search risk occurs when optimization creates an inaccurate representation of the business. Common examples include keyword stuffing the business name, publishing fake addresses, creating duplicate map listings, misrepresenting service areas, or distributing conflicting name-address-phone information across directories.

These tactics can make it harder for search systems to determine which information represents the real restaurant. They can also create customer confusion when people encounter several versions of the same business.

Reputation Risk

Restaurant reputation can be damaged by fake or purchased reviews, selective review gating, hostile responses to criticism, fabricated testimonials, or disclosure of private customer information.

The objective of reputation management should be to understand genuine customer feedback and respond appropriately, not manufacture the appearance of universal satisfaction.

Content Accuracy Risk

Content becomes risky when published information no longer matches restaurant operations. Examples include unavailable menu items, incorrect prices, outdated hours, unsupported dietary claims, or incorrect contact information.

Because local restaurant decisions are often immediate, even a small inaccuracy can affect a customer attempting to visit, call, order, or evaluate a meal.

Advertising Risk

Misleading promotions, artificial scarcity, false awards, unsupported rankings, or discounts that do not match the terms presented to customers can undermine credibility. Marketing teams should be able to verify meaningful promotional claims before publishing them.

Entity Trust Risk

Search engines, maps, directories, restaurant platforms, and customers all use overlapping information to understand a business. When the restaurant's name, phone, address, menu, hours, and services vary significantly across channels, the entity becomes less coherent.

Consistency should not mean copying identical marketing paragraphs everywhere. It means keeping foundational business facts stable while allowing useful platform-specific descriptions.
